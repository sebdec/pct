This document tells my [Pacific Crest Trail](https://www.pcta.org/) thru-hike, travelled from April 18 to July 23, 2026. I left Campo on the Mexican border and reached the Northern Terminus on the Canadian border 96 days later. The track is approximately 2,656 miles, or 4,274 kilometres and more than 140,000 metres of positive elevation. The course and photos are also available on [Polarsteps](https://www.polarsteps.com/SebastienDecreme/25177443-united-states).

I met people from all walks of life: men and women, young people and older people, with very different paths, levels and rhythms. This diversity reinforced the idea that most people could complete the PCT, provided that they progressed at their own pace and gradually, both physically and mentally. Distances can impress at first, but the body and mind adapt. You have to learn to listen, while remaining attentive to the advice and experience of other hikers.

The PCT in a few words

The Pacific Crest Trail is a great hiking trail in western United States. It links the Mexican border to the Canadian border through California, Oregon and Washington State. Approximately 2,650 miles long, or 4,265 kilometres.

A thru-hike consists of traveling the entire trail during the same season. Most hikers go north, or NOBO, to cross the desert in the spring, the Sierra after the main snows, then Oregon and Washington before the arrival of autumn. Beyond walking, the experience is based on continuous logistics: water, resupply, weather, snow, fire, food, equipment and access to cities.

I share 2 principles that I find interesting:

- [**Leave No Trace**](https://lnt.org/why/7-principles/) invites everyone to minimize their impact on nature.

- **Hike Your Own Hike** recalls that everyone travels the trail in their own way, according to their objectives, rhythm and choices.
